🏥 Diabetes Health Guide

Understanding diabetes criteria and what your measurements mean

⚡ Quick Reference: Diabetes Diagnosis Criteria

✅

Normal

  • Fasting Glucose: Less than 100 mg/dL
  • A1C: Below 5.7%
  • Random Glucose: Less than 140 mg/dL
⚠️

Prediabetes

  • Fasting Glucose: 100-125 mg/dL
  • A1C: 5.7% to 6.4%
  • Random Glucose: 140-199 mg/dL
🔴

Diabetes

  • Fasting Glucose: 126 mg/dL or higher
  • A1C: 6.5% or higher
  • Random Glucose: 200 mg/dL or higher

📊 Understanding Each Measurement

1

Blood Glucose (Sugar)

Blood glucose is the main sugar found in your blood. It comes from the food you eat and is your body's main source of energy.

Normal (Fasting): 70-99 mg/dL
Prediabetes: 100-125 mg/dL
Diabetes: ≥126 mg/dL
💡 What affects it:
  • Food intake (especially carbohydrates)
  • Physical activity
  • Stress levels
  • Medications
  • Illness or infection
2

Blood Pressure

Blood pressure measures the force of blood against artery walls. High blood pressure is common in people with diabetes and increases heart disease risk.

Normal: Less than 120/80 mm Hg
Elevated: 120-129/80 mm Hg
High (Hypertension): ≥130/80 mm Hg
💡 How to maintain healthy BP:
  • Reduce salt intake
  • Exercise regularly (30 min/day)
  • Maintain healthy weight
  • Limit alcohol consumption
  • Manage stress
3

Body Mass Index (BMI)

BMI is a measure of body fat based on height and weight. Higher BMI is associated with increased diabetes risk.

Underweight: Less than 18.5
Normal: 18.5-24.9
Overweight: 25-29.9
Obese: ≥30
📐 Calculate your BMI:

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m)]²

Example: 70 kg ÷ (1.75 m)² = 22.9 (Normal)

4

Insulin Levels

Insulin is a hormone that helps glucose enter cells for energy. In diabetes, the body doesn't make enough insulin or can't use it effectively.

Normal (Fasting): 2.6-24.9 μU/mL
Insulin Resistance: Above 25 μU/mL
💡 Signs of insulin resistance:
  • Increased hunger
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Weight gain (especially belly fat)
  • Fatigue after meals
  • Dark skin patches (acanthosis nigricans)
5

Age & Family History

Age and genetics play important roles in diabetes risk. The Diabetes Pedigree Function measures genetic influence based on family history.

🔴 High Risk Factors:
  • Age: Risk increases after 45 years
  • Family History: Parent or sibling with diabetes
  • Ethnicity: African American, Hispanic, Native American, Asian American
  • Previous Gestational Diabetes: During pregnancy
  • PCOS: Polycystic ovary syndrome

🚨 Warning Signs of Diabetes

💧

Increased Thirst

Feeling unusually thirsty, even after drinking water

🚽

Frequent Urination

Needing to urinate more often, especially at night

😴

Extreme Fatigue

Feeling tired and weak despite adequate rest

👁️

Blurred Vision

Changes in vision or difficulty focusing

🍽️

Increased Hunger

Feeling hungry even after eating

⚖️

Unexplained Weight Loss

Losing weight without trying

🩹

Slow Healing

Cuts and bruises take longer to heal

🦶

Tingling/Numbness

In hands or feet (neuropathy)

🛡️ Prevention & Management Tips

🥗

Healthy Diet

  • Choose whole grains over refined
  • Eat plenty of vegetables
  • Limit sugary drinks and foods
  • Control portion sizes
  • Include lean proteins
🏃

Regular Exercise

  • At least 150 min/week moderate activity
  • Mix cardio and strength training
  • Take breaks from sitting
  • Find activities you enjoy
  • Start slowly and build up
⚖️

Weight Management

  • Aim for 5-10% weight loss if overweight
  • Track your progress
  • Set realistic goals
  • Get support from family/friends
  • Celebrate small victories
🩺

Regular Check-ups

  • Annual physical exams
  • Blood sugar testing as recommended
  • Eye exams yearly
  • Foot checks regularly
  • Monitor blood pressure

👨‍⚕️ When to See a Doctor

🚨 Seek Immediate Medical Attention If:

  • Blood sugar above 300 mg/dL
  • Severe dehydration
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Confusion or loss of consciousness
  • Fruity-smelling breath
  • Severe abdominal pain

📅 Schedule an Appointment If:

  • You have multiple diabetes symptoms
  • Family history of diabetes
  • BMI over 25 with risk factors
  • Age 45+ (for screening)
  • Previous prediabetes diagnosis
  • Gestational diabetes history
